The first week of August brought a concentrated wave of 17 zoning applications across three boroughs — Brooklyn, Queens, and Staten Island — with Manhattan notably absent from the week's filings. Brooklyn dominated the docket with 9 applications spanning from Gravesend to Downtown Brooklyn, continuing a pattern of outer-borough rezoning activity driven by Mandatory Inclusionary Housing (MIH) designations. Of the 17 projects, 11 include MIH mapping, underscoring the city's ongoing reliance on the inclusionary housing framework to drive residential density increases. The week's largest project by unit count is the 250-unit, 18-story mixed-use development proposed at 1550 Bedford Avenue in Crown Heights (CD 9), while 783 Fourth Avenue in Sunset Park (CD 7) adds another 218 units across 13 stories — together representing nearly 470 new housing units entering the pipeline in a single week.

Staten Island's two contributions are both institutional — a Family Court consolidation in St. George and a DCP-led open space boundary amendment in the South Richmond district — marking a departure from the residential rezoning pattern that defines the Brooklyn and Queens filings. Geographically, the Brooklyn projects cluster heavily in the central and southern portions of the borough (CDs 2, 3, 4, 5, 7, 9, 14, and 15), while Queens activity centers on the central and eastern neighborhoods (CDs 2, 6, 7, and 10). Notably, every Queens project involves an MIH designation, reinforcing the pattern of transit-adjacent corridors being upzoned with affordability requirements attached.
