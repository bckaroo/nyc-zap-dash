Weekly Zoning Watch

NYC Zoning Application Portal — July 30–August 5, 2026

The first week of August brought 17 zoning applications into the City's pipeline, with a striking concentration in Brooklyn — nine of the seventeen filings, more than half the week's total. Queens followed with five projects, Staten Island contributed two, and Manhattan saw a single but notable theater-related text amendment. The Bronx was absent from this week's filings. The geographic pattern skews heavily toward outer-borough residential rezonings, with a clear corridor of activity along Brooklyn's southern and central neighborhoods (Bedford-Stuyvesant, Bushwick, Borough Park, Gravesend, Midwood, and Coney Island) and Queens' central and eastern districts (Long Island City, Forest Hills, Corona, and Flushing).

Mandatory Inclusionary Housing (MIH) dominates the week's agenda: twelve of the seventeen projects propose mapping new MIH areas, continuing a sustained citywide push to pair upzoning with affordable housing commitments. The week's largest proposal by unit count is 1550 Bedford Avenue in Crown Heights North — a 250-unit, 18-story mixed-use building seeking a C8-2 to R9A/C2-4 rezoning — followed by 783 Fourth Avenue in Greenwood Heights at 218 units. On the status front, five applications are newly noticed and opening public comment windows, while twelve have advanced into active public review. Notably, three projects involve conversions from manufacturing to residential or mixed-use zoning (M1-1 to M1-4A, M1-1 to M1-4/R6A, and M1-1 to R6A/M1-2A), reflecting ongoing pressure to transition industrial land to residential use along Brooklyn's former manufacturing corridors.
